\chapter{Results and Discussion}\label{ch:results}

\section{Results and Analysis}\label{sec:results}
OpenTrading was evaluated with version 4.7 of opentrading-benchmark \parencite{group2026opentradingbenchmark}, which compares it with two general-purpose harnesses, OpenCode and DeepSeek Harness (DSH), on the same model and the same data.

\subsection{Benchmark Setup}\label{sec:bench-setup}
The benchmark holds 300 tasks, questions in Vietnamese written as a user would ask them, with a required answer format.
They fall into five groups (\cref{tab:taskset}).
To keep the correct answers fixed, the data tools answer from a frozen snapshot recorded on 1 November 2026, with 30 October 2026 as the last trading session, and every correct answer was computed from that snapshot by a script.
Investor tasks assume a purchase at the close of 30 October 2026 and are scored with the closing prices of 23 November 2026, a period in which the market fell.

Each task was run once in each harness, \num{900} trials in total, under the shared configuration of \cref{tab:bench-config}.
Each harness kept its own system prompt and tool design, since these are what a harness contributes.
To give no harness an advantage outside its design, all three:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item saw only the same data tools, with OpenTrading's tool search switched off;
  \item ran their commands in the same isolated sandbox;
  \item saw a clock set to the snapshot date;
  \item had their questions to the user answered automatically in the same way.
\end{enumerate}

A trial counts as correct only if all four conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the judge accepts the answer against the gold answer;
  \item every number lies within the tolerance of the gold value;
  \item a task whose correct answer is a refusal receives no number;
  \item a manual review does not reject it (the review may reject a trial but never accept one).
\end{enumerate}
Investor tasks are scored by a fixed rule on the later prices, and a trial without a final answer counts as incorrect.
Trials that failed for infrastructure reasons, such as a broken network connection, were run again, and judge replies that could not be read were judged again.
Before the run, the judge classified 200 controlled answers, correct ones and copies made wrong in value, scale or refusal, all as expected (Cohen's $\kappa = 1.0$).

\subsection{Accuracy}\label{sec:accuracy}
OpenTrading answered 279 of the 300 tasks correctly (93.0\%), DSH 266 (88.7\%) and OpenCode 245 (81.7\%), as \cref{tab:accuracy} and \cref{fig:accuracy} show.

On compute, lookup and robustness tasks the three harnesses were within three tasks of each other.
The difference came from memory: OpenTrading applied the rules that a user had given in an earlier session, including rules later updated or withdrawn, in 58 of 59 tasks, against 50 for DSH and 30 for OpenCode.
On the investor tasks all three were weak, near one half.

Compared on the same tasks with the sign test of \cref{eq:sign-test}, OpenTrading was ahead of OpenCode on 40 tasks and behind on 6 ($p=3.1\times10^{-7}$), and ahead of DSH on 19 and behind on 6 ($p=0.015$).
Both differences are unlikely if the harnesses were equally good.

\subsection{Reliability and Cost}\label{sec:stability-cost}
OpenTrading returned an answer in a valid format in 299 of 300 trials, never refused a task that had an answer, and had one answer rejected for a number outside the tolerance, against 15 for OpenCode and 5 for DSH (\cref{tab:reliability}).
No harness gave a number on a task whose correct answer is a refusal.
OpenTrading also cost about half as much as the other two.

\subsection{Error Analysis}\label{sec:error-analysis}
Twelve tasks defeated all three harnesses, all but one in the investor group: four stock picks, four buy, hold or sell calls and three other picks, and one financial-statement calculation.
Three tasks were failed by OpenTrading alone.
No harness found the stock that would rise most in the next three weeks (0 of 4 for all), below the one-in-six rate of a random pick.
On the buy, hold or sell calls OpenTrading scored 7 of 16, OpenCode 10 and DSH 8, all below the 11 of 16 of a rule that always answers sell; OpenTrading's calls split into 8 sell, 6 buy and 2 hold, close to the others, so it lost on the choice for each company rather than by leaning towards buying.
In the memory group, OpenCode often used up its request limit before answering or applied a rule that the user had already replaced.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}\label{sec:threats}
Five limits apply to these results:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Every task ran once in each harness, so the results do not show how stable the answers are across runs.
  \item For 119 of the 300 tasks, the OpenCode and DSH answers come from the previous release of each harness, run with the same model, snapshot and judge but without the shared sandbox.
  \item The memory tasks, where most of the difference lies, were written by the group that built OpenTrading's memory, and their form may suit its design.
  \item The investor tasks cover one falling market over three weeks, so they are a signal about this period, not about investment skill.
  \item The judge was checked only on clearly correct and clearly wrong answers.
\end{enumerate}
